How the score works (transparent method)

  1. Digit values. Each digit carries a traditional weight: 8 (+10), 6 and 9 (+8), 2 (+5), 3 (+4), 1 (+3), 0 (+2), 5 (+1), 7 (0), 4 (−10). The average sets the base score.
  2. Famous combos. We scan for 100+ well-known sequences, longest first. Positive examples: 168 "prosper all the way", 518 "I will prosper", 888. Negative examples: 14 "want to die", 748, 250 "idiot".
  3. Patterns. Memorable patterns earn bonuses: repeated endings (…5555), ABAB, rising runs, and finishing on 8, 6 or 9. Extra 4s are penalised.
  4. Verdict. 85+ 大吉 Great Fortune · 70+ 吉 Lucky · 50+ 中平 Neutral · 35+ 小凶 Slightly Unlucky · below 35 凶 Unlucky.

Checking phone numbers

In China, mobile numbers ending in 8888 or 6666 are auctioned by carriers for large sums, and "no-4" numbers carry a premium. When reading aloud, 1 is pronounced yāo (幺). That turns 1 into a near-homophone of 要 "want" in combos like 518 and 168. Focus on the last four digits: they are the part people remember and judge.

Checking license plates and house numbers

For plates, ignore letters. Only digits count. For addresses, many buyers avoid any 4, especially 14 and 24 in Cantonese-speaking communities. Unit 8 or floor 8 often commands higher prices in Vancouver, Sydney, Singapore and Hong Kong.

Checking domain names

Chinese users find numeric domains easier to type and remember than pinyin. That's why numeric brands like 163.com, 360.com and 58.com exist. Six-digit .coms with no 0 and no 4 form the "premium 6N" class, and repeating patterns sell for more. Read our numeric domains guide.

Is my number really lucky or unlucky?

Number luck is a cultural belief, not science. It still has real social and economic effects: people pay more for lucky numbers and avoid unlucky ones. Use the score as cultural insight.

Does the order of digits matter?

Yes. Combos are read left to right like words, so 518 (I will prosper) differs from 815. The ending matters most for phone numbers and prices.
