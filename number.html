How Chinese numbers get their meanings

Mandarin has relatively few syllables and many homophones, so numbers easily "sound like" words. 8 (bā) echoes 发 (fā, prosper). 4 (sì) echoes 死 (sǐ, death). Strung together, digits become sentences: 5201314 reads as wǒ ài nǐ yī shēng yī shì, "I love you for a lifetime".

Mandarin vs Cantonese

Some meanings are regional. In Cantonese, 3 (saam) sounds like 生 (life), 2 like 易 (easy), and 5 (ng) like 唔 (not). That's why 58 can read as "won't prosper" in Hong Kong but is neutral in Beijing.

Numbers as internet slang

On WeChat, Weibo and in games, numbers work as shortcuts: 666 (awesome), 233 (LOL), 88 (bye), 555 (crying). Browse them all in our slang decoder and dictionary.
